# How to write this
- **Ground everything in the evidence.** Their dialogue is the strongest signal: their
vocabulary, register and preoccupations are right there. If they speak formally, say
so. If all they have is a shop greeting, that itself tells you they are a working
tradesperson, not a hero.
- **Invention is allowed, but only to connect evidence.** A background that explains why
a Redguard woman is working in a Riften tavern is good. Inventing that she is secretly
a Thalmor agent is not - it will contradict the game.
- **Never invent quest outcomes, secret identities, or relationships with named
characters the evidence does not mention.** Other NPCs have their own profiles and
will not corroborate your invention.
- **Faction names are evidence.** A faction naming a place or trade tells you where they
belong and what they do.
- Write plainly and specifically. No purple prose, no fantasy-novel throat-clearing.
- Third person, present tense.
- If the evidence is thin, write a **short, plausible, ordinary** person. A believable
minor character beats an invented adventurer.
# Output format
Output EXACTLY the ten sections below, each introduced by its `###` heading on its own
line, in this order, with nothing before the first heading and nothing after the last
section. No preamble, no commentary, no markdown fences.
### summary
Two or three sentences: who they are, what they do, where. This is read most often.
### interject_summary
One sentence in the form "Torg interjects when: X; Y; Z" - the specific
subjects that would make this person speak up unprompted.
### background
A short paragraph of history that explains how they came to be here doing this.
### personality
A short paragraph: temperament, manners, what they value, what irritates them, how they
treat strangers.
### appearance
Two or three sentences on how they look and carry themselves. Use their race, sex and
what they wear. Describe only what someone in the room could see.
### aspirations
Three to five bullet points, each starting with "- ", of what they actually want. Keep
them proportionate to the person.
### relationships
Bullet points, each starting with "- ", in the form "- Name: relationship and feeling".
Only people, factions or gods the evidence supports. If there is nothing to go on, give
the one or two ties their job and factions imply.
### occupation
One or two sentences on what they do day to day.
### skills
Three to six bullet points, each starting with "- ", of what they are actually good at.
Ordinary competence is fine and usually correct.
### speech_style
A short paragraph on how they talk: register, accent, sentence length, verbal habits.
If their dialogue above shows a distinctive voice, describe THAT voice specifically
rather than a generic one.
